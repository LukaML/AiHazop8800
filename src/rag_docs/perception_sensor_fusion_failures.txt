PERCEPTION & SENSOR FUSION FAILURE MODES (AVP/APA CONTEXT)

Purpose:
- Catalog specific sensor failure modes and fusion algorithm weaknesses
  relevant to automated valet parking and automated parking assist features.
- Provide concrete failure mechanisms, parameter ranges, and environmental
  triggers for HAZOP cause generation.


=== CAMERA SYSTEMS ===

Configuration: typically 4 fisheye cameras (190-degree FOV each) mounted
at front bumper, rear bumper, and both side mirrors, producing a surround-
view (bird's-eye) composite image.

Failure modes:

  Lens soiling: road splash, dust, and condensation deposit on lens surface.
  Accounts for approximately 35% of camera-related field failures. Soiling
  degrades contrast ratio below detection thresholds. Partial soiling causes
  asymmetric detection zones — one quadrant detects obstacles while the
  opposite quadrant is blind. Built-in lens heaters mitigate condensation but
  not mud or road salt deposits.

  Calibration drift: thermal cycling (repeated -20C to +60C exposure) causes
  camera mounting bracket micro-shifts. Extrinsic calibration errors as small
  as 0.3 degrees produce 5-10 cm object position errors at 3 m range. These
  errors compound in the bird's-eye stitching algorithm, creating phantom gaps
  or overlaps between camera views.

  Saturation and bloom: direct sunlight or headlight reflection saturates the
  CMOS sensor. High-dynamic-range (HDR) mode reduces but does not eliminate
  saturation. At 100,000+ lux, the auto-exposure algorithm oscillates between
  overexposed and underexposed frames, causing intermittent detection dropout
  lasting 100-300 ms per cycle.

  Rolling shutter artifacts: CMOS sensors with rolling shutter expose rows
  sequentially (typical 15-30 ms full-frame readout). During vehicle motion
  or vibration, vertical structures appear skewed. At parking speeds (3-5 km/h),
  skew is typically 2-5 pixels — enough to distort parking line detection and
  narrow-gap width estimation.

  Stitching artifacts: the bird's-eye view is composited from four camera
  streams. Objects near camera view boundaries (especially tall objects like
  pillars, pedestrians, or open doors) exhibit duplication, stretching, or
  disappearance in the stitching zone. A pedestrian standing at a stitching
  boundary may appear as two half-bodies or vanish entirely from the composite.

  Parking line detection failure: worn, faded, or non-standard parking markings
  reduce line detection confidence. Detection degrades below 60% recall when
  paint contrast ratio drops below 30%. Wet surfaces reflect surroundings onto
  markings, further reducing contrast. Unusual marking colors (yellow on light
  concrete) or non-standard line widths also reduce detection accuracy.


=== ULTRASONIC SENSORS ===

Configuration: typically 12 sensors — 4 front, 4 rear, 2 per side. Operating
frequency 40-52 kHz. Maximum reliable range 3-5 m (varies by sensor model).
Minimum detection range 15-30 cm (dead zone).

Failure modes:

  Membrane contamination: ice, mud, or road salt deposits on the transducer
  membrane attenuate acoustic output by 6-12 dB. Partial contamination shifts
  the beam pattern, creating angular blind spots. Heavy contamination prevents
  transmission entirely, appearing identical to "no obstacle detected."

  Cross-talk between adjacent sensors: when two or more sensors fire
  simultaneously or within their echo window, one sensor receives echoes from
  another sensor's transmission. Cross-talk produces phantom obstacles at
  incorrect distances. Time-division multiplexing mitigates this but increases
  total scan cycle time to 50-80 ms per full sweep.

  Sound-absorbing surfaces: soft materials (clothing, foam bumpers, rubber
  mats, shopping cart fabric covers) absorb ultrasonic energy, reducing echo
  amplitude below detection threshold. This creates false-negative detection —
  the sensor reports "clear" when an obstacle is present. Children wearing
  thick winter coats are particularly vulnerable due to clothing absorption.

  Extreme weather performance: ultrasonic detection reliability degrades
  significantly in heavy rain (water droplets scatter acoustic energy), strong
  wind (>40 km/h shifts echo paths), and extreme cold (<-20C increases air
  absorption). Combined failure rate increases by approximately 73% in severe
  winter conditions compared to nominal 20C dry conditions.

  Temperature-dependent speed of sound: air temperature affects sound velocity
  (331 m/s at 0C vs 349 m/s at 30C). If the sensor firmware uses a fixed
  speed-of-sound value, distance calculations contain 3-5% error across the
  operating temperature range. This produces systematically over- or under-
  estimated obstacle distances.


=== LiDAR ===

Configuration: typically 16-channel solid-state or mechanical scanning LiDAR,
905 nm wavelength, 100-200 m range in clear conditions, 10-20 Hz scan rate.
Some parking systems use flash LiDAR with shorter range (10-30 m) but higher
angular resolution.

Failure modes:

  Rain and fog scatter: water droplets in the air scatter the 905 nm beam.
  Heavy rain (>25 mm/hr) reduces effective range by approximately 60%. Dense
  fog (<100 m visibility) can reduce range to under 10 m, making the LiDAR
  effectively blind beyond the nearest obstacle.

  Point cloud sparsity: at close range in parking scenarios (1-5 m), the 16-
  channel LiDAR produces only 3-8 points on a pedestrian-sized object. This
  sparse representation makes classification unreliable — a child (0.5 m wide)
  may register as only 1-2 points, falling below the minimum cluster size for
  obstacle detection.

  Sun saturation at 905 nm: direct sunlight contains significant energy at
  905 nm. When the sun is within or near the sensor's field of view, ambient
  light saturates the photodetector. This affects specific angular sectors
  rather than the entire scan, creating directional blind zones that shift
  with sun position.

  Retroreflective interference: highly reflective surfaces (license plates,
  safety vests, road signs) produce saturated returns that overwhelm the
  receiver's dynamic range. The resulting range measurement may be severely
  shortened or extended, misrepresenting obstacle distance by meters.


=== RADAR (77 GHz mmWave) ===

Configuration: typically 1-2 corner radar modules for parking applications,
77 GHz band, 0.3-100 m range, angular resolution 5-15 degrees.

Failure modes:

  Multipath in parking structures: metallic surfaces (steel beams, columns,
  metal fences, car bodies) reflect radar signals, creating multipath echoes.
  In metallic parking structures, multipath produces ghost targets — apparent
  obstacles at locations where no physical object exists. Ghost targets appear
  at the sum of direct and reflected path distances.

  Ghost targets from adjacent vehicles: radar signals penetrate gaps between
  parked vehicles and reflect off objects in adjacent bays. The radar
  interprets these returns as obstacles in the driving corridor. This effect
  is most pronounced with narrow parking bays (2.3-2.5 m width).

  Doppler ambiguity at ultra-low speed: at parking speeds below 2 km/h, the
  Doppler frequency shift approaches the radar's minimum detectable velocity.
  Stationary and very slow-moving objects become indistinguishable, degrading
  the ability to differentiate a moving pedestrian from a parked vehicle.

  Bumper fascia attenuation: radar signals must pass through the vehicle's
  bumper fascia. Metallic paint, embedded heating wires, or water film on the
  inner surface can attenuate the signal by 3-8 dB. Wet conditions increase
  attenuation, reducing maximum detection range.


=== SENSOR FUSION ALGORITHMS ===

Architecture: parking systems typically use either late fusion (each sensor
produces its own object list, fused at the track level) or mid-level fusion
(feature-level fusion before classification). Some systems use occupancy grid
fusion where each sensor votes on grid cell occupancy.

Failure modes:

  Temporal desynchronization: sensors operate at different frame rates (camera
  30 fps, ultrasonic 12-20 Hz, radar 13-20 Hz, LiDAR 10-20 Hz). If timestamp
  alignment exceeds 50 ms error, a moving pedestrian at 5 km/h is displaced
  by 7 cm between sensor frames. This displacement creates association errors —
  the fusion algorithm may treat one pedestrian as two separate objects or fail
  to associate detections across sensors.

  Conflicting detections: one sensor reports an obstacle while another reports
  clear space at the same location. Common causes include sensor-specific
  material properties (radar sees metal, camera sees glass differently) and
  field-of-view differences. The conflict resolution policy determines whether
  the system favors detection (safe but may cause unnecessary stops) or non-
  detection (unsafe — may miss real obstacles).

  Association errors: the data association algorithm matches detections across
  sensors using position and velocity predictions. In cluttered parking
  environments with many similarly-sized targets, the algorithm may incorrectly
  associate detections — merging two close pedestrians into one track, or
  splitting one vehicle into multiple tracks.

  Occupancy grid stale data: grid-based fusion assigns occupancy probability
  to each cell. Cells outside current sensor coverage retain their last-known
  probability until decay timeout (typically 0.5-2 seconds). During this
  window, a moved obstacle (e.g., pedestrian who walked away) remains marked
  as occupied, or a newly appeared obstacle (pedestrian who walked in) remains
  marked as free.

  Confidence threshold miscalibration: the fusion algorithm uses confidence
  thresholds to decide when detections are reliable enough to act on. Thresholds
  calibrated for highway driving (high-speed, large objects, long range) may be
  inappropriate for parking (low-speed, small objects, short range). Overly
  aggressive thresholds suppress weak but valid parking-relevant detections.

  Late vs mid fusion architecture tradeoffs: late fusion is simpler but cannot
  recover information lost in per-sensor processing (e.g., if a camera DNN
  misclassifies a child as a bollard, late fusion propagates the error). Mid-
  level fusion can potentially correct such errors but is more complex and
  requires precise sensor calibration.

  Training data gaps: deep learning perception models trained primarily on
  highway datasets may lack adequate representation of parking-specific objects:
  shopping carts, low bollards, wheel stops, children at atypical heights,
  pets, and unusual obstacle geometries. This manifests as reduced detection
  recall or misclassification in parking scenarios.
