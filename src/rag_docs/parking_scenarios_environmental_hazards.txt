PARKING SCENARIOS & ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS (AVP/APA CONTEXT)

Purpose:
- Document the operational design domain (ODD) for automated valet parking
  and parking assist features, including facility types, environmental
  conditions, obstacle types, and hazardous scenarios.
- Provide scenario-specific context for realistic HAZOP effect generation.


=== PARKING FACILITY TYPES ===

Surface lot (outdoor):
  Open-air parking with painted markings on asphalt or concrete. Full GPS
  availability. Subject to all weather conditions. Typical slot dimensions
  2.4 m x 5.0 m (perpendicular) or 2.0 m x 6.0 m (parallel). Marking
  degradation from UV exposure and tire wear reduces detection confidence.
  Surface irregularities (potholes, speed bumps 50-75 mm height, expansion
  joints) create suspension disturbances that affect sensor stability.

Multi-story parking structure:
  Concrete or steel-frame enclosed structure with multiple levels connected
  by ramps. Clearance height typically 2.1-2.4 m (limiting roof-mounted
  sensor placement). Ramp gradient 8-16% with transition angles at top and
  bottom. Sharp turns (180-degree switchbacks with 5-7 m inner radius) at
  ramp transitions. Structural pillars at 5-8 m intervals create occlusion
  zones. Metallic reinforcement and steel beams cause radar multipath.
  Fluorescent or LED lighting with variable intensity (100-500 lux) and
  potential flicker at 100-120 Hz affecting camera exposure.

Underground parking:
  Below-grade structure with no GPS reception. Relies entirely on dead
  reckoning, visual SLAM, or infrastructure positioning. Typically lower
  ceiling height (2.0-2.2 m). Poor natural lighting requires full reliance
  on artificial lighting. Fluorescent lamp flicker can cause camera image
  banding. Damp conditions promote ultrasonic membrane condensation. Cell
  phone signal attenuation affects remote parking control via smartphone.
  Entry/exit ramp transitions from daylight to darkness create severe
  camera exposure adaptation challenges (2-5 second adaptation time).

Residential / private:
  Private driveway or garage with non-standard layouts. No painted markings.
  Narrow driveways (2.5-3.5 m width). Garage openings as narrow as 2.4 m
  with clearance as low as 2.0 m. Uneven surfaces, drainage grates, garden
  hoses, and toys as unexpected obstacles. No infrastructure positioning
  support.


=== ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS ===

Rain:
  Light rain (<5 mm/hr): camera lens water droplets cause localized image
  blur. Ultrasonic membrane water film attenuates echo amplitude by 3-6 dB.
  Wet markings reduce detection contrast by 40-60%.
  Heavy rain (>25 mm/hr): LiDAR range reduced by 60%. Camera images severely
  degraded. Ultrasonic detection range reduced by 30-50%. Standing water on
  surface creates specular reflections confusing camera-based detection.
  Braking distance on wet surface increases by 50-100% at parking speeds.

Snow and ice:
  Snow accumulation on sensors: 2-5 mm of snow on ultrasonic membrane
  blocks transmission entirely. Snow on camera lens creates opaque patches.
  LiDAR aperture blocked by snow/ice buildup.
  Icy surface: braking distance increases by 3-10x compared to dry surface.
  At 10 km/h on ice, stopping distance extends from 1 m to 3-10 m. Tire
  slip during steering maneuvers causes trajectory deviation. Wheel odometry
  becomes unreliable due to wheel spin/slip.
  Snow-covered markings: parking line detection impossible. System must rely
  entirely on obstacle-based space detection or infrastructure guidance.

High temperature (>40C ambient):
  EPS motor thermal derating begins at 100C winding temperature, reached
  more quickly in hot ambient. ECU operating temperature approaches limits
  (typically 85-105C junction). Camera CMOS sensor thermal noise increases,
  reducing image quality. Tire pressure increases (approximately 1 psi per
  5.5C), affecting wheel circumference and odometry accuracy.

Low temperature (<-20C ambient):
  Brake fluid viscosity increases significantly, slowing pressure build
  time by 20-50%. Power steering fluid (in hydraulic systems) thickens,
  increasing assist lag. Battery capacity reduction (20-40% at -20C) limits
  available power for electric drive creep and EPS. Ultrasonic sensor
  membrane stiffens, shifting resonant frequency and reducing sensitivity.
  Rubber seals and bushings harden, increasing friction and reducing
  responsiveness.

Sun glare:
  Direct sunlight entering camera lens causes flare, bloom, and local
  saturation. Worst case occurs at low sun angles (early morning, late
  afternoon) when light enters nearly horizontally — common in surface lots.
  Transition between sun and shadow (e.g., entering parking structure) causes
  2-5 second camera adaptation delay. LiDAR 905 nm sensors experience
  background saturation when facing directly into sun.

Darkness:
  Camera detection relies on artificial lighting. In poorly lit areas
  (<50 lux), camera performance degrades significantly. Headlamp illumination
  creates harsh shadows and specular reflections. Pedestrians in dark
  clothing are particularly difficult to detect. Ultrasonic and radar
  performance is unaffected by darkness.


=== OBSTACLE TYPES ===

Pedestrians:
  Adults (typical cross-section 0.5 m x 0.3 m, height 1.5-1.9 m): detected
  reliably by camera and LiDAR above 5 m range. Ultrasonic detection reliable
  below 3 m. Walking speed 1.2-1.5 m/s in parking areas.
  Children (cross-section 0.3 m x 0.2 m, height 0.8-1.3 m): significantly
  smaller radar and LiDAR cross-section. Below ultrasonic sensor beam height
  in some configurations. Camera detection relies on shape recognition, which
  may fail for partially occluded children (e.g., between parked vehicles).
  Wheelchair users, stroller pushers: lower detection profile, slower movement
  speed, less ability to evade.

Vehicles:
  Parked vehicles: stationary, large radar cross-section, reliable detection.
  Primary hazard is door opening into the path of the automated vehicle.
  Door opening speed 0.5-1.0 m/s, extending 0.8-1.2 m from vehicle body.
  Reversing vehicles: another vehicle backing out of an adjacent space while
  the automated vehicle maneuvers. Relative closing speed up to 10 km/h.
  Motorcycles and bicycles: smaller cross-section, potentially below camera/
  LiDAR mounting height. Kickstand-parked motorcycles may topple if contacted.

Fixed obstacles:
  Bollards: steel or concrete posts, 0.1-0.3 m diameter, 0.5-1.0 m height.
  Small radar cross-section. May be below LiDAR scan plane.
  Curbs: 50-150 mm height change at parking space perimeter. Detectable by
  ultrasonic and LiDAR but may be below camera detection threshold if not
  painted. Curb strike at 5 km/h can damage wheels, tires, and suspension.
  Pillars: structural columns in parking structures, typically 0.3-0.6 m
  diameter. Create radar multipath and camera occlusion zones.
  Wheel stops: concrete blocks 100-150 mm height, 1.5-2.0 m length. May be
  detected as curb or missed entirely if below sensor beam.

Transient obstacles:
  Shopping carts: lightweight, irregular shape, semi-transparent to radar.
  May roll if contacted at low speed. Height 1.0 m, width 0.6 m.
  Construction barriers: temporary, non-standard geometry. Orange cones
  (0.3-1.0 m height), water-filled jersey barriers, temporary fencing.
  Animals: pets (dogs on leash or loose), birds, small wildlife. Irregular
  motion patterns, unpredictable trajectory.


=== MANEUVER PARAMETERS ===

Speed envelope: 0-15 km/h for all parking maneuvers. Typical target speed
3-7 km/h during space approach and maneuver execution. Emergency braking
target: full stop within 0.5-1.5 m from 10 km/h.

Clearance requirements: lateral (side) clearance to obstacles typically
30-50 cm. Front and rear clearance to obstacles typically 50-80 cm. These
margins account for sensor accuracy, control precision, and path tracking
error. Clearance below 20 cm triggers emergency stop in most systems.

Completion time: perpendicular parking 30-60 seconds. Parallel parking
40-90 seconds. Multi-point maneuver adds 10-20 seconds per correction.
Systems typically abort if maneuver exceeds maximum time (120-180 seconds).

Steering dynamics: full lock-to-lock steering typically requires 2.5-3.5
turns. At parking speed, lock-to-lock time is 2-4 seconds. EPS motor
reaches thermal limit after 3-5 consecutive full-lock maneuvers.


=== HAZARDOUS SCENARIOS ===

Child darting between parked vehicles: a child emerges from between two
parked vehicles into the path of the automated vehicle. Detection time is
severely limited — the child may be visible for only 0.3-0.5 seconds before
reaching the vehicle's path. At 5 km/h, the vehicle covers 0.7-1.0 m in
the 0.5 seconds between detection and brake actuation.

Ramp rollback: the vehicle stops on a parking structure ramp (8-16%
gradient). If the brake releases before sufficient drive torque is
established (EPB auto-hold release logic), the vehicle rolls backward.
On a 12% gradient, a 2000 kg vehicle accelerates at approximately 1.2 m/s^2,
reaching 4 km/h within 1 second of brake release.

Pillar contact during tight turn: the vehicle executes a turn in a multi-
story structure with pillars at 5-8 m spacing. The rear overhang (0.8-1.2 m
behind rear axle) sweeps outward during the turn. If the path planner
underestimates rear overhang sweep, the rear corner contacts a pillar.
Contact at 3-5 km/h causes bumper and quarter-panel damage.

Adjacent vehicle door opening: while the automated vehicle passes a parked
vehicle, an occupant opens their door into the driving corridor. The door
extends 0.8-1.2 m from the parked vehicle's body. If lateral clearance is
less than the door swing radius, contact occurs. Detection of the door
opening requires camera or LiDAR with <200 ms response time.

Wet ramp loss of traction: on a wet or icy parking structure ramp, the
traction tires lose grip during acceleration or braking. ABS and traction
control may not be calibrated for the ultra-low speeds used in parking
maneuvers. The vehicle slides sideways on a curved ramp, contacting the
guardrail or adjacent vehicle.

Pedestrian in blind zone at maneuver start: a pedestrian stands in the
ultrasonic dead zone (15-30 cm from bumper) when the automated vehicle
begins a parking maneuver. The ultrasonic sensors cannot detect obstacles
within their minimum range. The vehicle begins moving toward the pedestrian
without detection.
